================================================================================
PATCHTST EXTENSION PLAN — "BEAT THE PAPER" ON ETTH1 T=96
================================================================================

Baseline (this repo, current main):
  ETTh1 L=336 T=96  ->  MSE 0.3851  MAE 0.4024
Paper target (PatchTST/42, Table 3):
  ETTh1 L=336 T=96  ->  MSE 0.3750  MAE 0.3990

We are 2.7% over on MSE, 0.85% over on MAE — already within the 10% band.
The bar for "beating the paper" is MSE < 0.375 AND MAE < 0.399.

State-of-the-art context (May 2026, public benchmarks for ETTh1, T=96):
  PatchTST/42 (2023, Nie et al.)     0.375 / 0.399
  TimesNet      (2023, Wu et al.)    0.384 / 0.402
  iTransformer  (2024, Liu et al.)   0.386 / 0.405
  TimeMixer     (2024, Wang et al.)  0.375 / 0.400
  PathFormer    (2024, Chen et al.)  0.380 / 0.402

Honest read: PatchTST/42 is hard to beat *cleanly* on this exact cell. Most 2024
papers tie or slightly under-perform it. So our extension story has two arcs:
  (a) close our 3% gap to the paper number (likely)
  (b) inch ahead via one or two well-motivated modifications (stretch)

A well-defended set of extensions that *don't* beat 0.375 is still a strong
deliverable — the rubric values careful experimentation and reflection over raw
numbers (final_deliverables.pdf, "Grading", bullets 2 and 3).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EVALUATION DISCIPLINE (apply uniformly across all extensions)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

To make comparisons trustworthy:

  - Same data pipeline (dataset.py, paper splits, train-only z-score) for every run.
  - Same seed (2021) for the headline number; report ±std over 3 seeds (2019,
    2020, 2021) in the report. Paper reports ±std over 5 seeds in §A.6.1.
  - Single change per experiment — never combine two ideas in the same run
    until each has been measured alone (so we can attribute gains).
  - Always log to results/logs/<extension_tag>.csv via the existing CSVLogger.
  - Always save the best checkpoint to results/checkpoints/<extension_tag>.pt.
  - Report on all four horizons T in {96, 192, 336, 720} for the report (full
    Table 3 row); just T=96 is enough for the poster if time-constrained.

================================================================================
TIER 1 — EASY GAP-CLOSERS (Sunday night / Monday morning, ~30 min each)
================================================================================

These directly address the deviations our own baseline review flagged. Goal:
close the 3% MSE gap to the paper, ideally land at 0.375 ± 0.005.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T1A. BatchNorm in the encoder block (replace LayerNorm)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  Paper uses BatchNorm inside the transformer block (footnote, p.5 of
  arXiv:2211.14730v2). Their §A.4.4 ablation attributes ~1-2% MSE to this
  choice on ETT datasets. Our use of LayerNorm (PyTorch's nn.TransformerEncoder
  default) is the single largest known deviation; fixing it should account for
  most of our 3% gap.

CITATION
  - Nie et al. 2023, ICLR — footnote on p.5: "Zerveas et al. (2021) has shown
    that BatchNorm outperforms LayerNorm in time series Transformer."
  - Zerveas et al. 2021, KDD — original Time Series Transformer (TST) paper.

IMPLEMENTATION
  Replace nn.TransformerEncoderLayer with a custom block that uses BN1d in
  place of LayerNorm. Skeleton in code/model.py:

    class BNTransformerEncoderLayer(nn.Module):
        def __init__(self, d_model, nhead, dim_feedforward, dropout):
            super().__init__()
            self.attn  = nn.MultiheadAttention(d_model, nhead, dropout=dropout,
                                               batch_first=True)
            self.ff    = nn.Sequential(
                nn.Linear(d_model, dim_feedforward), nn.GELU(),
                nn.Dropout(dropout),
                nn.Linear(dim_feedforward, d_model),
            )
            self.bn1   = nn.BatchNorm1d(d_model)
            self.bn2   = nn.BatchNorm1d(d_model)
            self.drop  = nn.Dropout(dropout)

        def forward(self, x):                           # x: (B, N, D)
            a, _ = self.attn(x, x, x, need_weights=False)
            x    = self.bn1((x + self.drop(a)).transpose(1,2)).transpose(1,2)
            f    = self.ff(x)
            x    = self.bn2((x + self.drop(f)).transpose(1,2)).transpose(1,2)
            return x

  Note: BN1d expects (B, C, L) — hence the transposes. Stack n_layers of
  these in place of nn.TransformerEncoder.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  -1 to -2% MSE (per paper's own ablation). Closes most of our gap.

EFFORT
  ~30 min coding, ~5 min training.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T1B. Full RevIN with learnable affine (γ, β)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  We currently do parameter-free instance norm (subtract mean, divide std,
  undo at output). The original RevIN paper (Kim et al. 2022) includes
  learnable per-channel affine scale γ and shift β applied AFTER normalization
  and BEFORE the model (and inverted at the output). The affine adds
  flexibility for the model to retain a per-channel scale if useful.

CITATION
  Kim et al. 2022, ICLR — "Reversible Instance Normalization for Accurate
  Time-Series Forecasting against Distribution Shift". OpenReview cGDAkQo1C0p.

IMPLEMENTATION
  Add two learnable parameters of shape (1, M, 1) inside model.py and apply
  them after subtract-mean-divide-std at input, invert at output.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  -0.5 to -1% MSE (paper's own RevIN ablation, §A.4.4 of arXiv:2211.14730v2,
  shows similar magnitude).

EFFORT
  ~15 min coding, ~5 min training.

================================================================================
TIER 2 — TRYING TO INCH AHEAD (Monday afternoon, ~1-2 hr each)
================================================================================

These borrow ideas from post-PatchTST forecasting work. Each has a defensible
mechanism for *why* it might help on ETTh1 specifically.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T2A. Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) replacing learnable additive pos_emb
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  Our baseline uses an additive learnable position embedding (1, N, D) added
  to patch tokens. RoPE encodes position by rotating Q and K vectors before
  attention; it (i) introduces no extra parameters, (ii) generalizes to longer
  sequences than seen at training, and (iii) has been the de-facto standard in
  modern transformers (LLaMA, GPT-NeoX, etc.). For time series, RoPE's relative-
  position semantics may model the *temporal distance between patches* better
  than absolute learnable embeddings.

CITATION
  - Su et al. 2021, "RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position
    Embedding", arXiv:2104.09864.
  - Used in LLaMA (Touvron et al. 2023), broadly adopted since.

IMPLEMENTATION
  Drop self.pos_emb. Inside the (custom) attention, rotate Q and K by RoPE
  matrices before the dot product. ~30 lines of standard code; reference
  implementations are everywhere.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  Small — 0 to -0.5% MSE. May help more on longer horizons (T=720) than T=96.
  Worth reporting as an honest negative result if it doesn't help.

EFFORT
  ~1 hr coding, ~10 min training.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T2B. Trend-residual decomposition + dual-stream patch transformer
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  ETTh1 has a strong yearly seasonal trend on top of higher-frequency residuals.
  PatchTST treats both as one signal. DLinear (Zeng et al. 2022) showed that
  decomposing into a moving-average trend + residual and modeling them
  separately helps a lot. We add the same decomposition to PatchTST: run two
  independent PatchTST encoders (shared weights or separate) on the trend and
  the residual, sum their forecasts, undo RevIN.

CITATION
  - Zeng et al. 2022, "Are Transformers Effective for Time Series Forecasting?",
    AAAI 2023 — DLinear / NLinear.
  - Wu et al. 2021, "Autoformer", NeurIPS — same decomposition, embedded in
    transformer block.

IMPLEMENTATION
  Add a moving-average-based decomposition (window=25 hours typical) at the
  top of forward(). Run two PatchTST forward passes, one per stream. Sum.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  -0.5 to -2% MSE. ETTh1's daily/weekly periodicity makes it a good fit for
  this. Could be the single biggest extension win.

EFFORT
  ~1.5 hr coding, ~10 min training.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T2C. iTransformer cross-channel attention as a complementary stream
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  PatchTST is strictly channel-independent: each of the 7 ETTh1 channels is
  processed in isolation. iTransformer (Liu et al. 2024) flips this, treating
  each variate as a token and attending across variates. On ETTh1 the load
  channels (HUFL, MUFL, LUFL) and the oil temperature OT are causally linked,
  so explicit cross-channel attention has at least a hypothesis. We *don't*
  expect iTransformer alone to beat PatchTST (it doesn't, in the numbers
  above), but a *fused* model that sums PatchTST and iTransformer logits could
  capture both inductive biases and out-perform either alone.

CITATION
  Liu et al. 2024, ICLR — "iTransformer: Inverted Transformers Are Effective
  for Time Series Forecasting".

IMPLEMENTATION
  Build a small iTransformer (just channel-as-token attention with one or two
  encoder layers and a Linear head producing 7 x 96). Train alongside or
  ensemble with PatchTST predictions: pred = α * patchtst + (1-α) * itransformer,
  with α tuned on val set.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  Variable. -0.5% MSE if the two models genuinely capture complementary
  signal; null result is also informative for the report.

EFFORT
  ~2 hr coding, ~15 min training each.

================================================================================
TIER 3 — TRAINING / GENERALIZATION TRICKS (Monday evening, ~30 min each)
================================================================================

Lower-risk ideas — won't break the model, may stack with Tier 1/2 wins.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T3A. Multi-seed averaging
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  Paper (§A.6.1) reports std over 5 seeds: ETTh1 T=96 mean MSE 0.376 ± 0.0012.
  A single-seed run can be off by ±0.005 MSE. Train 5 seeds, ensemble at
  inference (average predictions, not weights).

CITATION
  Standard practice; PatchTST §A.6.1.

IMPLEMENTATION
  for seed in [2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023]: train, save preds.
  Final = mean of saved preds.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  -0.2 to -0.7% MSE from variance reduction alone. Cheap.

EFFORT
  5x training time (so ~10 min), ~10 min code.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T3B. Output-space test-time augmentation (TTA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  At inference, predict not just for window starting at t but also at
  t-1, t-2, ..., t-k. Average the k+1 predictions for the overlapping
  forecast horizon. Costs k+1x compute at eval, free regularizer.

CITATION
  Standard TTA in vision; rarer in TS — would be a small original contribution.

IMPLEMENTATION
  In _run_epoch's eval branch, for each batch construct k shifted copies and
  average predictions where they overlap.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  -0.1 to -0.5% MSE. Diminishing returns past k=4.

EFFORT
  ~30 min coding, no extra training.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T3C. Hyperparameter sweep on patch_len, stride, dropout, lr
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HYPOTHESIS
  Paper §A.4.1 sweeps patch length P in {2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40} and
  finds P=16 is *not always* best — for ETT specifically the curve is fairly
  flat. We sweep more aggressively over (P, stride, dropout, lr) on val and
  pick the best configuration before reporting on test.

CITATION
  PatchTST §A.4.1 (varying patch length).

IMPLEMENTATION
  Small grid: P in {8, 12, 16}, stride in {4, 8, 12}, dropout in {0.1, 0.2, 0.3},
  lr in {5e-5, 1e-4, 2e-4}. ~3*3*3*3 = 81 runs is too many; prune to ~20 with
  Optuna or random search.

EXPECTED IMPACT
  -0.3 to -1% MSE. Risk: overfitting val set. Mitigate by reporting best-on-val
  config and its test number, not best-on-test.

EFFORT
  ~30 min coding, hours of GPU time. Run overnight if you want.

================================================================================
TIER 4 — STRETCH (only if Tier 1+2 finish early)
================================================================================

T4A. SSM/Mamba block as a drop-in replacement for transformer encoder
     - Uses state-space model instead of self-attention. Faster, often
       comparable on long sequences. Reference: Gu & Dao 2023.
     - Major architectural surgery; only attempt if you have a full afternoon.

T4B. Continuous wavelet transform features
     - Compute scalogram per channel, concatenate as extra input features.
     - Captures multi-resolution structure that patches alone miss.

T4C. Transfer learning: pre-train on Electricity, fine-tune on ETTh1
     - Paper §4.2 / Table 5 already shows this works. Re-running it on our
       impl is a clean reproduction-of-extension.
